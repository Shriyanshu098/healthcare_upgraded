📚 Altitude Illness Safety Guide

Evidence-based clinical guidelines from CDC & Wilderness Medical Society.

Rule #1: Assume AMS First

Any unexplained sickness above 2,500m is Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) until proven otherwise.

Rule #2: Never Ascend Sick

Never climb higher if you have active symptoms of headache, nausea, or dizziness.

Rule #3: Descend When Worsening

If symptoms worsen at rest or breathing difficulty develops, descend 500 to 1,000 meters immediately.

Comparison of Common Altitude Conditions:

Condition Altitude Key Symptoms Primary Action
AMS (Acute Mountain Sickness) > 2,500m Headache, nausea, fatigue, dizziness Stop ascending, rest 24-48h, hydrate
HAPE (Pulmonary Edema) > 3,000m Severe breathlessness at rest, blue lips, cough Immediate descent (≥ 1,000m), oxygen
HACE (Cerebral Edema) > 3,500m Loss of balance/coordination, confusion Immediate emergency descent, medical help
